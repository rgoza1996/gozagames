Simulation mechanics

How heroes shop.

Every purchase respects town stock, personal gold reserves, skill proficiencies, and cultural identity.

Progression

Heroes who invest in themselves

Lords, mercenary captains, and AI party heroes steadily upgrade their gear as campaigns unfold and sell outgrown equipment back to the market.

Player clan

Less clan micromanagement

Let leaders of your clan’s parties and caravans shop for their own upgrades while you retain full control over your character and main-party companions.

Identity

Distinct cultural styles

Keep gear faithful to a hero’s, clan’s, or kingdom’s culture—or let heroes adopt foreign equipment—configured independently for armor, weapons, and mounts.

Loadouts

Sensible ranged equipment

Heroes avoid mixing incompatible ranged weapon types, pair bows and crossbows with matching quivers, and prioritize replacing missing arrows or bolts.

Full wardrobe

Mounts & civilian kits

Shopping plans cover warhorses, harnesses, battlefield armor, and civilian town outfits so heroes stay equipped on and off the field.

Customization

Granular MCM options

Tune shopping frequency, spending limits, mounted vs. foot preferences, and optional purchase notifications in Options → Mod Options → Nobles Buy Stuff.

Setup

Requirements & load order.

Required dependencies

Enable these four standard Bannerlord community frameworks above Nobles Buy Stuff in the launcher, using a single installation method (either Steam Workshop or Nexus Mods / manual):

  • Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord v1.4.8 or newer
  • Harmony Load before ButterLib
  • ButterLib Load before UIExtenderEx
  • UIExtenderEx Load before MCM
  • Mod Configuration Menu (MCM v5) Load before Nobles Buy Stuff

Campaign & support notes

You can enable Nobles Buy Stuff on an existing campaign save without restarting; your configured mod settings persist across sessions.

For bug reports, compatibility notes, or balance feedback, post on the Nexus Mods page, the Steam Workshop listing, or email rogiegoza@gmail.com.
